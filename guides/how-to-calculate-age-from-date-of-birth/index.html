How to Calculate Age from Date of Birth

ToolNova Guides · 7 minute read

Subtracting one year from another looks like the whole job — until a form asks for an exact age in years, months and days, and the birthday hasn't happened yet this year. Age arithmetic is calendar arithmetic: leap years, months of four different lengths, and the one rule everyone forgets. This guide works through the method step by step with real examples, including the February 29 birthday and the age-on-a-deadline calculation that school admissions and visa forms actually need.

The Method in 3 Steps

Age is completed years since birth, counted on the calendar — not a division of total days. The method every official form uses is:

Step 1 — Subtract the years. Current year minus birth year. Born in 1990 and it is 2026: 2026 − 1990 = 36.

Step 2 — Check the birthday. If this year's birthday has not happened yet, subtract one year. This is the step people skip, and it is the single most common age error. A December birthday means you carry the younger age for almost the whole year.

Step 3 — Count months, then days. From the most recent birthday, count the full calendar months that have passed, then the remaining days. Months are calendar months, not 30-day blocks — the stretch from 15 June to 15 November is exactly 5 months, whether those months had 30 or 31 days.

That is the whole method. Everything else in this guide is the edge cases — and the edge cases are where the marks, and the form rejections, live.

Worked Example 1: A Birthday That Already Passed

Someone born on 15 June 1990. What is their age on 28 November 2026?

Step 1: 2026 − 1990 = 36. Step 2: the birthday, 15 June, has passed — no subtraction. Step 3: from 15 June to 15 November is 5 full months; from 15 November to 28 November is 13 days.

Answer: 36 years, 5 months and 13 days. In other units that is 437 months, 1,902 weeks, 13,315 days and about 319,560 hours alive. They were born on a Friday, and their next birthday — 15 June 2027 — is 199 days away.

Worked Example 2: The Birthday Hasn't Happened Yet

Someone born on 10 December 1998. What is their age on 28 November 2026?

Step 1: 2026 − 1998 = 28. Step 2: the birthday, 10 December, is still 12 days away — subtract one year, so 27. Step 3: the last birthday was 10 December 2025; from there to 10 November 2026 is 11 months, and to 28 November is 18 more days.

Answer: 27 years, 11 months and 18 days — 10,215 days in total. Anyone who stops at Step 1 writes down 28 and is wrong by a full year for the next 12 days. This is the error that costs people a school place or an age-limited competition entry: "turns 28 in December" is not the same as "is 28".

Worked Example 3: Born on February 29

Leap-day birthdays are the one case where the calendar itself has no answer, because in three years out of four there is no 29 February at all. The convention almost every calculator and official form uses is to treat 1 March as the birthday in non-leap years — it is the first day the person is unambiguously a year older. (Some families celebrate on 28 February instead. Either is a convention; the important thing is to use one consistently, because the two choices disagree by a day.)

Someone born on 29 February 2000. On 28 February 2026 they are 25 years, 11 months and 30 days — their birthday has not arrived under the 1 March convention. On 1 March 2026 they turn 26 exactly. They have lived 9,497 days, including six February 29s after their birth (2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024). Their next real leap-day birthday is 29 February 2028 — 730 days after 1 March 2026.

Worked Example 4: Age on a Future Date (Deadlines)

Forms rarely ask "how old are you today?" — they ask how old someone will be on a cutoff date: a school term start, an exam day, a visa appointment. The method is identical; only the "current" date changes.

A child born on 10 March 2015. How old will they be on 1 September 2030, the first day of term?

Step 1: 2030 − 2015 = 15. Step 2: the birthday, 10 March, has passed by September — no subtraction. Step 3: 10 March to 10 August is 5 months; to 1 September is 22 more days.

Answer: 15 years, 5 months and 22 days — 5,654 days old on that date. If the school's cutoff is "must be 15 on 1 September", this child qualifies with five months to spare; a child born 10 September 2015 would be 14 years, 11 months and 22 days — and would wait a year.

The Leap-Year Rules (and Why 1900 Wasn't One)

Total-day counts only work if leap years are right, and the rule has a twist most people forget:

  • Divisible by 4 → leap year. 2024 was one; 2028 will be.
  • Except century years — years divisible by 100 are not leap years. 1900 was not a leap year, and 2100 will not be one.
  • Except centuries divisible by 400 — those are leap years. 2000 was a leap year, and 2400 will be.

The Gregorian calendar averages 365.2425 days a year, which is why the rules exist. A person born in 1990 has lived through nine February 29s by their 36th birthday (1992, 1996, 2000, 2004, 2008, 2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024) — and a day-count that ignores them is nine days short.

Age in Other Units

Forms ask for years, but milestones and curiosity ask for other units. All of these describe the same person from Worked Example 1 — born 15 June 1990, measured on 28 November 2026:

36 years · 437 months · 1,902 weeks · 13,315 days · 319,560 hours

Two milestones worth knowing: your 10,000th day alive arrives at about 27 years and 4 months — for someone born 1 January 2000, that day is 19 May 2027. And most 30-year-olds have passed 10,000 days without noticing, because nobody counts days until a calculator shows them the number.

How to Calculate Age in Excel or Google Sheets

If the ages live in a spreadsheet — a class list, a team roster, an HR sheet — do it with a formula instead of by hand. Put the birth date in cell A2:

=DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"Y") — full years
=DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"YM") — remaining months
=DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"MD") — remaining days

Combined into one cell: =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"Y")&" years, "&DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"YM")&" months, "&DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"MD")&" days"

Replace TODAY() with any fixed date — a cutoff, an exam day — to get the age on that date. The same three formulas work in Google Sheets. One warning: DATEDIF is undocumented in Excel (it works, Microsoft just never put it in the function list), so it won't appear in autocomplete — type it in full.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Subtracting the years and stopping there. 2026 minus 1998 is 28 — but until the December birthday passes, that person is 27. The birthday check is the whole difference between right and wrong.
  • Multiplying years by 365 for total days. 36 × 365 = 13,140, but the true count for those 36 years is 13,149 — nine February 29s. Every leap day lived through adds a day the multiplication misses.
  • Dividing total days by 365.25 and calling it an age. It gives a decimal, not a years-months-days split, and it drifts — the real Gregorian average is 365.2425 days, so the 365.25 shortcut gains about a day every 128 years. Fine for a rough guess, useless for a form.
  • Counting months as 30-day blocks. The stretch from 15 June to 15 November is 5 months, not 150 days divided by 30. Calendar months have 28 to 31 days, and the count must follow the calendar.
  • Mixing up date formats. 03/04/2005 is March 4 in the US and April 3 almost everywhere else. Use a date picker, and check the result reads the way you intended.
  • Forgetting time zones on the birth date. Age is a calendar-date question, and the date on the birth certificate is the one that counts — a baby born at 11:30 PM in New York on 10 December was born on 11 December in London. For forms, the registered date wins.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate age from date of birth?
Use three steps. First, subtract the birth year from the current year. Second, if this year's birthday has not happened yet, subtract one more year. Third, count the full months and days since the last birthday. Example: born 15 June 1990, on 28 November 2026 that gives 36 years, 5 months and 13 days.

How old am I if I was born in 1990?
It depends on your birthday, not just the year. Someone born on 15 June 1990 is 36 on 28 November 2026, because their birthday has passed. Someone born on 10 December 1990 is still 35 on that date and turns 36 on 10 December. The year alone is never enough — the month and day decide.

How do you calculate age if your birthday hasn't happened yet this year?
Subtract one year from the simple year difference. Born 10 December 1998, on 28 November 2026 the year subtraction gives 28, but the birthday is still 12 days away, so the real age is 27 years, 11 months and 18 days. Forgetting this step is the most common age-calculation mistake.

How do you calculate age for someone born on February 29?
In a non-leap year there is no February 29, so most calculators and official forms treat 1 March as the birthday. Someone born 29 February 2000 is 25 on 28 February 2026 and turns 26 on 1 March 2026. Some families celebrate on 28 February instead — either date is a convention, so pick one and use it consistently.

How many days old am I?
Count the days between your birth date and today, including every February 29 you have lived through. Someone born 15 June 1990 has lived 13,315 days by 28 November 2026. A quick 365-times-years estimate undercounts by one day for every leap day lived — nine days for that person — so use a real date difference, not multiplication.

How do I calculate age in Excel or Google Sheets?
Put the birth date in cell A2 and use =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"Y") for full years, =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"YM") for the remaining months and =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"MD") for the remaining days. Combined: =DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"Y")&" years, "&DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"YM")&" months, "&DATEDIF(A2,TODAY(),"MD")&" days". The same formulas work in Google Sheets; replace TODAY() with a fixed date to get the age on that date.

Does your age change on January 1 or on your birthday?
On your birthday. Age counts completed years since birth, so you turn a year older on the anniversary of your birth date, not at New Year. Someone born in December is the younger age for almost the whole calendar year. A few cultures count differently — for example counting a newborn as one year old — but official forms everywhere use the birthday rule.
