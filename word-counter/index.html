How to Use the Word Counter

  1. Type directly into the box, or paste text from an essay, article, resume or social media draft.
  2. Watch the counters update in real time — no button press needed.
  3. Check the reading time if you are preparing a speech, presentation or video script.
  4. Edit your text until it fits the limit you need, then copy it back to your document.

Who Needs a Word Count?

Students use it to hit essay and assignment lengths (a typical college essay is 500–1,000 words). Writers and bloggers use it to keep articles scannable — most readers finish pieces that take under 7 minutes. Job seekers trim resumes to one page, and social media managers fit posts into character limits: 280 characters on X (Twitter), 2,200 on Instagram captions and about 3,000 on LinkedIn posts. Public speakers rely on the speaking-time estimate so a 10-minute talk lands at roughly 1,300 words. Whatever you write, knowing the count before you submit saves embarrassing overruns and last-minute cuts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I count words in my essay?

Paste or type your essay into the box above. The word count, character count and reading time update instantly as you type — there is no button to press.

Does it include spaces in the character count?

It shows both: total characters including spaces, and characters without spaces, so you can meet either kind of limit.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time uses an average adult speed of about 200 words per minute; speaking time uses about 130 words per minute.

Is my text uploaded anywhere?

No. Counting happens entirely in your browser. Your text never leaves your device.
