vCard QR Code Generator

Let people save your contact in one scan — no typing your number, no misspelled name, no lost business card. Fill in your details, print the code, and put it on your card, badge, or shop counter. Free, instant, 100% local in your browser · no sign-up.

Why a vCard QR Code Beats Handing Over a Number

Every networking conversation ends the same way: someone asks for your number, you recite it, they type it into their phone — and one digit goes wrong, or the name gets saved as "Emma Realtor??" and is never found again. A vCard QR code ends that ritual. The other person points their phone camera at the code, taps "Add to Contacts", and your name, number, email and website land in their address book exactly as you wrote them.

It also survives the moment better than a paper card. Business cards get left on tables, washed in pockets, and typed in later — if ever. A scanned contact is saved on the spot, searchable the next day, and it carries your website too, so the person who met you at a trade show can actually find you again on Monday morning.

How a vCard QR Code Works

  1. Type your name and at least one way to reach you — a phone number or an email. Add your company, job title and website if you want them on the card.
  2. Click "Generate vCard QR Code". The tool builds a vCard — the standard contact format (BEGIN:VCARD … END:VCARD) that iPhones and Android phones both understand — and turns it into a QR code.
  3. Test it with your own phone camera before printing: it should show your contact card with a Save button. Then download the PNG and print it wherever people meet you.

Behind the scenes, the code stores a short text block in vCard 3.0 format — for example BEGIN:VCARD, VERSION:3.0, N:Rodriguez;Emma, FN:Emma Rodriguez, TEL;TYPE=CELL:+1 555 014 8820, EMAIL:emma@rodriguezrealty.com, END:VCARD (each field on its own line) — with commas, semicolons and backslashes escaped so the phone reads every field exactly. A Worked Example

A real-estate agent, Emma Rodriguez of Rodriguez Realty, meets buyers at open houses all weekend. Instead of handing out cards that get lost, she prints a small sign for the welcome table: "Scan to save my contact" with her vCard QR code, plus the same code on the back of her business card.

Result: visitors scan and save her number, email and website in about five seconds — no dictation, no typos, no card to lose. On Monday she gets calls from people whose names she never wrote down, because her contact was already in their phones. When she changes brokerages, she generates a fresh code and swaps the sign — a two-minute job.

Tips for vCard QR Codes That Save First Time

  • Include the country code on your phone number. A number without +1 (or your country's code) may not dial correctly for someone visiting from another region. Write it exactly as you would want it dialed.
  • Keep it to the essentials. Name, phone, email, website — and company and title if they matter. A code stuffed with a postal address, notes and social links gets denser and harder to scan; the essentials scan fast and save cleanly.
  • Test on both an iPhone and an Android phone if you can. Both read vCard codes from the built-in camera, but the save screen looks different on each — check that your name and number appear correctly on both.
  • Print at 1024 × 1024 for anything on paper. Small downloads look fine on screen but go soft when printed; the large size keeps the squares crisp on cards, badges and signs.
  • Keep the quiet zone. The white border around the code is part of the code — don't crop it off and don't print on a dark or busy background.
  • Tell people what the code does. A bare code on a card gets ignored. One line — "Scan to save my contact" — turns it from decoration into a saved contact.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the name off and only encoding a number. A number-only code saves as an unnamed contact nobody can find later. The name is the whole point — always fill it in.
  • Putting a personal email on a public code. A code printed on a shop sign or banner is public. Use a work email and a business number for public codes, and keep private details for codes you share one-to-one.
  • Forgetting to update the code when details change. A static code never expires, but it also never updates itself. New number, new job, new website — generate a fresh code and replace the old prints.
  • Printing too small or on a glossy, reflective surface. Tiny codes and glare both defeat phone cameras. Print at a readable size, on matte paper where possible, and test-scan the actual print.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I create a QR code for my contact details?

Type your name, phone number and email above (plus job title, company and website if you want them), and click Generate. The code stores all of it as a vCard — the standard contact format phones understand — so anyone who scans it can save you to their contacts in one tap.

What happens when someone scans a vCard QR code?

Their phone camera reads the code and shows your contact card, with an Add to Contacts or Save button. On iPhones and most Android phones this works from the built-in camera app — no special app needed. They still choose whether to save it; nothing is added without their tap.

Is a vCard QR code better than a normal QR code with my phone number?

Yes, for sharing contact details. A plain-text code just shows your number on screen and the other person still has to type it in. A vCard code fills in the name, number, email and website fields for them, so nothing gets mistyped and nothing gets lost.

Does a vCard QR code expire?

No. The code is static — it keeps working as long as you keep using the same details. If you change your phone number or job, generate a fresh code and replace the old print.

Is my contact information uploaded or stored anywhere?

No. The code is generated entirely in your browser and the details never leave your device. That also means the code itself is the only copy — if you lose the print, just generate it again from the same details.

Where should I put a vCard QR code?

On a business card, a name badge, a shop counter sign, an email signature image, or a trade-show banner — anywhere someone meets you and would otherwise ask for your number. Pair it with a short line like "Scan to save my contact" so people know what it does.

What size should I print a vCard QR code?

For a business card scanned at arm's length, about 2.5 cm (1 inch) square is enough. For a poster scanned from a few steps away, go bigger — a useful rule is 10:1, so a code scanned from 1 meter away should be at least 10 cm wide. Download at 1024 × 1024 for print.
