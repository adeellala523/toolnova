Why Designers Still Reach for Fake Latin

Placeholder text solves a real problem: real copy is distracting. Show a client a website mockup filled with their actual draft text and they'll spend the meeting proofreading instead of looking at the layout. Show them a headline that says “HEADLINE GOES HERE” and the design looks broken and empty. Lorem ipsum threads the needle — it has the rhythm, word lengths and texture of real prose, so spacing, typography and hierarchy can be judged honestly, while carrying no meaning anyone can argue with.

That's why, five centuries after it first appeared in print, it remains the default filler for designers, developers, typesetters and template builders. This generator gives you three useful granularities: full paragraphs for body copy, sentences for cards, testimonials and captions, and words when you need an exact length for a headline or button-adjacent blurb. You can start with the canonical opening or dive straight into varied text, generate as much as you need, and copy it in one click.

A 30-Second History of Lorem Ipsum

Here's the part most people never learn: lorem ipsum isn't gibberish, and it isn't random. It's scrambled from a real book — Cicero's de Finibus Bonorum et Malorum (“On the Ends of Good and Evil”), written in 45 BC. The famous opening, “Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet…”, is a chopped-up fragment of “dolorem ipsum” — “pain itself” — from a passage about pleasure and suffering.

A printer in the 1500s scrambled the text to make a type specimen book, and it has been the printing industry's dummy text ever since. It survived letterpress, phototypesetting, and desktop publishing — Letraset sheets popularized it in the 1960s — before Aldus PageMaker carried it into the digital era. When you generate it here, you're using the same filler that demonstrated Gutenberg-era typefaces. (Probably.)

Tips for Using Placeholder Text Well

  • Match real-world lengths. Generate roughly as much text as the final copy will have. A design that looks balanced with two lines of lorem can fall apart under a real six-line paragraph.
  • Mix your units. A realistic page needs paragraphs for the body, sentences for cards and captions, and a handful of words for headings. Generating all three from here keeps the texture consistent.
  • Test the ugly cases too. Fill one section with a single very long paragraph and another with one short sentence. Layouts should survive both, because real content will eventually do both.
  • Never ship it. Set a hard rule: lorem ipsum in a live product is a bug. It slips through embarrassingly often — search your site for “lorem” before every launch.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Presenting lorem-filled designs without saying so. Clients have approved mockups thinking the Latin was a styling choice. A ten-second explanation at the start saves a confused email later.
  • Using it where meaning matters. Navigation labels, buttons, error messages and forms should always be designed with real words — usability lives there, and dummy text hides real problems like a checkout button that needs the word “Checkout”.
  • Generating far more than you need and trimming randomly. Cut mid-sentence and your mockup quietly looks sloppy. Generate the right amount instead — that's what the Words and Sentences options are for.
  • Telling clients it's “just Greek”. It's Latin — and now you know whose. (The saying is “it's all Greek to me”; the filler is Cicero.)

Frequently Asked Questions

What does “lorem ipsum dolor sit amet” mean?

It's a scrambled fragment of Cicero, not a clean sentence, so it doesn't translate exactly. The recognizable piece, “dolorem ipsum”, means “pain itself”, from a passage discussing pleasure and pain in de Finibus (45 BC). The scrambling is deliberate — the point is text that looks like language without communicating anything.

How much text can I generate?

As much as you need — set the amount and unit and generate again for more. Paragraphs for long-form layouts, sentences for components, words for precise lengths like headlines and labels.

Can I use lorem ipsum in a real, published project?

As temporary filler during design and development, absolutely — that's its purpose. Just replace it with real content before anything goes live; published lorem ipsum is a classic and very visible oversight.

Why not just type “text text text” or repeat a sentence?

Repeated words create visible patterns that distort how a layout reads — every line looks identical, so spacing and typography can't be judged fairly. Lorem ipsum's varied word lengths mimic real prose, which is precisely its value.

Is this the traditional version of the text?

Yes — it draws on the standard lorem ipsum vocabulary and sentence patterns in circulation since the 1500s, including the classic opening when you keep the start option ticked.

Do designers still use lorem ipsum with real content available?

Many do for early stages, then switch to realistic draft content as a project matures. Lorem ipsum is best for judging pure layout and typography before copy enters the conversation — use it early, replace it deliberately.
