GTA 6 Beginner Tips: Things to Do First in Leonida

These GTA 6 beginner tips cover the things to do first in GTA 6 now that Grand Theft Auto VI is out — it launched on Thursday, November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Use the starter checklist below, then read the full GTA 6 starter guide: how to save, how to keep your cash safe, how the new wanted system works, and how to play as Lucia and Jason. All facts checked on November 20, 2026 against launch guides.

Before You Press Start

Free up 212 GB or more. The GTA 6 pre-load was about 212 GB, so clear space before you download — you need that much plus room for updates and save data. If your console is nearly full, delete a game you have finished rather than starting the download and hitting a wall halfway through.

Install the game and the day-one update, then lock down your accounts. A launch this big attracts phishing, so turn on two-step verification on PlayStation Network, Xbox and Rockstar Games, and use a unique password for each account. Our Password Generator makes a strong one in a click. Never download a "GTA 6 PC" file or APK — there is no PC version at launch, and those files are fake.

Play the story first. Launch is the single-player story only — there is no multiplayer in GTA 6 at launch, and Rockstar has not announced a GTA VI Online date. The story follows Lucia Caminos and Jason Duval in Leonida, and it is the whole game right now. If online play is the reason you are buying, wait for Rockstar's announcement. The game scored Metacritic 99 on PS5, so the story is a very good place to start.

Your First Hours in the Leonida Keys

The story starts in the Leonida Keys, the island chain south of Vice City — Chapter 1 stays there, with Jason living in Key Lento. Take your time here: the early missions are deliberately slow, and the serious money only opens up later in the story, so there is no rush to get rich in the first hours. Explore the Keys, learn the roads, and get comfortable with the controls before Vice City opens up.

Get Jason's safehouse in order. Early on, Jason is at his safehouse in Key Lento. Fix it up ahead of Lucia's arrival — repairs to the roof, walls and paint are yours to do — and look around for vehicles you may have stashed. In the bathroom you can change your appearance, and the punching bag and weights build stamina. Before you get bank access, there is a cash stash under the loose floorboards in Jason's bedroom: use it, because cash you carry is lost if you die or are arrested.

Try both protagonists. The prologue is Lucia's, then you play as Jason on his own for the first couple of hours until Lucia gets out and joins him. From then on, most story missions let you swap between the two mid-mission or play a whole mission as either one — but some missions lock you to one character for story reasons. When the two are together, you can give your partner contextual commands, so learn to use them both instead of sticking to one.

How to Save in GTA 6

There is an autosave feature, but do not rely on it alone. To save manually, hit pause, go to Game and select Save Game — you have multiple save slots, so keep a spare slot for big decisions. You cannot save in the middle of a mission, so save between missions and before anything risky. One warning from launch guides: you cannot replay missions — once a mission is finished, that is your only go at it — so make a manual save at the start of each chapter.

Money: Bank It or Lose It

Do not carry a lot of cash. Cash you are carrying disappears if you are killed or arrested, so deposit it as soon as you can. Early in the story you get the BuckMe app and a bank account — use it to deposit your funds and carry $1,000 or less with you. Until the bank opens up, use the floorboard stash in Jason's bedroom. The bank balance is shared between Jason and Lucia.

Check a business before you rob it. Before a holdup, check the map for the take value and the security level, and stick to low-security targets until both protagonists can work together. Stolen goods are not money yet either — jewellery, drugs, marked cash and cars go to specialised fences, who unlock as the story progresses.

Crime Works Differently in GTA 6

Witnesses matter. A crime is only reported if someone sees it, a victim reports it, or an alarm or camera catches it — no witness, no police. A one-star wanted level means the police do not know who you are yet, so leave the area before they arrive and do not escalate.

The police remember what you look like. Officers track your clothes, hat and glasses, your car, and even the gun in your hand. To lose them, change your outfit — use the spare outfits in your personal vehicle's trunk — and if they know your car, ditch it and split up. A respray helps too. Scan a car with your phone before you steal it: the scan shows its value, its security system and whether it has a tracker on it.

Your criminal profile is watching. The game tracks how you commit crimes: clean, controlled work builds a Professional reputation, while needless violence drags it down — and killing police or civilians can make it far worse. Stealth takedowns and non-lethal shots count in your favour from the very first mission.

Stay Alive: Eat, Sleep, Shower, Work Out

GTA 6 is a simulation as much as an action game. Your characters have three core stats — Health, Stamina and Focus (Focus slows down time so you can aim your shots) — and eating, sleeping, showering and working out keep them topped up. The boost from sleeping, exercising, eating and showering lasts for your gameplay session or until your character dies. Working out builds health, cardio builds stamina, and sleeping at home skips the night — handy when a mission only opens at a certain hour. If Jason and Lucia go to the gym together, both get the benefits.

Explore Leonida: the Map, the Minimap and Fast Travel

Learn the map early. The world map itself tells you which buildings you can enter: darker blue buildings have an entrance or interior, lighter blue ones do not. Hundreds of interiors are enterable, and your minimap shows hidden rooms and enterable doors when you get close — use Focus mode on a door to check it, too. If your minimap keeps fading out, check the settings: you can lock it on screen, and set distance, temperature and weight units to the ones you are used to.

Use your phone and use autopilot. Your phone's apps handle banking, vehicle delivery and ride-hailing — RydeMe is the closest thing to fast travel, and paying extra skips the ride. For long drives, hold down on the D-pad for cinematic camera mode, which activates autopilot, or ask your partner to take the wheel when you are travelling together. And listen to the world around you: a random world event can turn into a windfall.

Difficulty and Settings Worth Changing First

There is no difficulty setting to pick in GTA 6 — the story plays at one set difficulty, as in GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2. What you can change is how hard it feels: the Gameplay tab in Settings has Accessible Combat (enemies have less health and deal less damage) and Reduced Vehicle Damage (your car takes less damage from crashes and gunfire), plus aiming options including lock-on targeting. Turn those on if you want an easier ride, and off again if things get too easy — neither locks you out of achievements or trophies. Common beginner mistakes: buying the game for online play on day one (there is no multiplayer yet); carrying all your cash instead of banking it; stealing a car without scanning it first; and trusting hour counts for the story — Rockstar has not announced a story length, so any number you see is a guess, not a fact.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first in GTA 6?

Free up 212 GB or more of storage, install the game and the day-one update, and turn on two-step verification on PlayStation Network, Xbox and Rockstar Games. Then play the story first — launch is single-player only — starting in the Leonida Keys. Learn to save manually, bank your cash with the BuckMe app as soon as you get it, and scan any car with your phone before you steal it.

How do I save in GTA 6?

Hit pause, go to Game and select Save Game. There is also an autosave feature, but a manual save lets you pick the exact moment, and you have multiple save slots. You cannot save in the middle of a mission, so save between missions and before anything risky. You cannot replay missions, so keep a manual save at the start of each chapter.

Is there multiplayer in GTA 6?

No — launch is the single-player story only. Rockstar has not announced a GTA VI Online date, and reports suggest multiplayer arrives in 2027. If you bought the game for online play, that part is still to come.

How long is the GTA 6 story?

Rockstar has not announced a story length for GTA 6, so there is no official number of hours. Any hour count you see online is a guess or an estimate from reviewers, not a confirmed figure. Play at your own pace and do not trust a specific length until Rockstar states one.

Should I play as Lucia or Jason first?

You do not choose — the story decides for you. The prologue is Lucia's, then you play as Jason on his own for the first couple of hours until Lucia gets out and joins him. After that, most story missions let you swap between the two mid-mission or play a whole mission as either one, so try both and use whichever fits the job.

Can I transfer GTA Online progress to GTA 6?

No — GTA VI Online has not launched, and Rockstar has not announced any transfer of GTA Online progress, characters or money to GTA 6. GTA 6 at launch is a separate single-player story with its own characters, Lucia and Jason, and its own economy. Do not trust any site offering a transfer tool.

What difficulty should I pick?

There is no difficulty setting to pick in GTA 6 — the story plays at one set difficulty, as in GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2. If you want an easier game, turn on Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage in the Gameplay tab of Settings, and use the lock-on targeting option. Neither assist locks you out of achievements or trophies.
