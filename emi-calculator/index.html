How to Use the Loan Calculator

  1. Enter the amount you plan to borrow (the price minus any down payment).
  2. Type the annual interest rate your lender quoted — check whether it is APR or a flat rate.
  3. Set the term in months: 60 for a typical 5-year auto loan, 360 for a 30-year mortgage.
  4. Compare scenarios — try a shorter term and watch total interest fall.

Why Total Interest Deserves Your Attention

Monthly payments hide the real price of a loan. Borrow $300,000 at 7% for 30 years and the payment is about $1,996 — but the interest alone totals roughly $418,000, more than the house price itself. Shortening the term to 15 years raises the payment to about $2,696 while cutting interest to about $185,000, saving over $230,000. The same math, smaller scale, applies to cars and personal loans: every extra year of term trades a lower payment for a higher total cost. Run two or three scenarios here before talking to a lender, and you will negotiate with your eyes open. This calculator uses the standard amortization formula and excludes taxes, insurance and fees, so treat results as close estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

An Equated Monthly Installment — the fixed monthly payment that repays a loan, part interest and part principal, by the end of the term.

How is the monthly payment calculated?

With P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where r is the monthly rate and n the number of payments.

Does a longer term save money?

It lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest — often dramatically on mortgages.

Can I use it for mortgages and auto loans?

Yes — any fixed-rate installment loan. Taxes, insurance and lender fees are not included.
