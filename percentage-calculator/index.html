Percentages in Real Life

Percentages run modern life: sale discounts and coupons, tips and sales tax at checkout, exam scores, interest on savings and credit cards, rent increases, battery levels and investment returns. They are powerful because they compare values fairly — a $20 discount means little until you know it is 40% off a $50 jacket or just 2% off a $1,000 phone. The trickiest daily case is change: if a stock rises 50% then falls 50%, it is not back where it started (100 → 150 → 75). Working through the three modes above a few times makes these numbers second nature.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. Example: 20% of 150 = 150 × 20 ÷ 100 = 30.

How do I find the percentage increase?

Subtract old from new, divide by the old value, multiply by 100. From 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.

How do I work out a discount price?

Find the percent of the price in mode 1, then subtract: 30% of $80 is $24 off, so you pay $56.

Percent vs. percentage points — what is the difference?

Going from 5% to 7% is +2 percentage points, which is a 40% relative increase. The words are not interchangeable.
