Why Passport Photos Get Rejected

Most passport-photo rejections have nothing to do with the camera. They fail on three things: wrong dimensions (a 4000-pixel phone photo uploaded as-is, or a square photo sent to a 35×45 mm form), wrong file size (the portal caps uploads at 50, 100 or 200 KB and the photo is 4 MB), and wrong framing (head too big, head cut off, or a busy background). This page fixes the first two automatically — exact dimensions and exact file size — and the tips below cover the third.

The pixel sizes come from the print standard of 300 DPI (dots per inch). At 300 DPI, one inch is 300 pixels and one millimetre is about 11.81 pixels. So a US 2×2 inch photo is 600×600 px, a UK 35×45 mm photo is 413×531 px, and a Canadian 50×70 mm photo is 591×827 px. Portals check these numbers, so getting them exactly right matters more than getting them roughly right.

How the Passport Photo Tool Works

  1. Pick your country's passport size above, then click "Choose a photo" and pick any JPEG, PNG or WebP. It starts processing the moment the photo loads.
  2. The tool crops the photo from the centre to the passport aspect ratio, then resizes it to the exact pixel size for that country — 600×600 for the US, 413×531 for the UK and EU, 591×827 for Canada.
  3. It then searches from high to low JPEG quality, keeping the best quality that still fits under your chosen file-size limit (50, 100 or 200 KB). The exact dimensions are never changed to hit the size — only the quality is.
  4. Check the result — size, dimensions, and a side-by-side preview — then hit "Download Passport Photo". Done in seconds.

JPEG is used for the output because PNG compression is lossless and can't hit an exact size reliably. Your photo is processed entirely in your browser: nothing is uploaded, stored, or seen by anyone.

Tips for a Passport Photo That Gets Accepted

  • Take the photo against a plain white wall. The tool resizes and compresses — it cannot change your background. A white wall in bright, even daylight is the single biggest acceptance factor.
  • Keep your head centred with space above it. The tool crops from the centre, so a head at the edge of the frame gets cut. For a US photo, your head (chin to top of hair) must be 1 to 1⅜ inches — 300 to 412 pixels at 300 DPI.
  • Face the camera straight on, neutral expression, eyes open. No smiling with teeth, no tilted head, no hair over the eyes. Glasses are allowed in most countries only if there is no glare on the lenses — safer to take them off.
  • Start from the original photo, not a re-saved copy. Every JPEG save discards detail; compressing an already-compressed file looks worse at the same file size. Always upload the freshest copy you have.
  • Check your portal's limit before choosing. The US online visa photo (DS-160) must be a JPEG between 600×600 and 1200×1200 px and under 240 KB; other portals commonly cap at 50, 100 or 200 KB. Pick the limit your form states.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uploading your passport photo to random "free" sites. Many photo tools send your file to their server and you have no idea what happens next. This tool runs entirely on your device — check any tool for that before trusting it with an ID photo.
  • Stretching a photo to fit. Squashing a landscape photo into a square distorts the face and gets rejected. This tool crops to the right shape instead of stretching — always crop, never stretch.
  • Using a selfie from arm's length. Close selfies distort the face (wide nose, uneven proportions) and usually fail the head-size rule. Ask someone to take the photo from about 1.5 metres away, or prop the phone up and use the timer.
  • Submitting a photo with shadows or a coloured background. A shadow on the wall behind you or a cream-coloured wall can both fail the plain-white-background rule. Bright, even light on a white wall fixes both.
  • Re-uploading the finished passport photo back through the tool. Re-encoding a compressed JPEG discards more detail each time. Compress once, from the original, and keep the master.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I compress a photo for a passport application?

Upload your photo above, pick your country's passport size (US 2×2 in, UK/EU 35×45 mm, Canada 50×70 mm), and choose your portal's file-size limit (50, 100 or 200 KB). The tool crops the photo to the exact passport dimensions and compresses it under the limit, at the best quality that still fits.

What size should a passport photo be in pixels?

At the standard print resolution of 300 DPI, a US passport photo (2×2 inches) is 600×600 pixels, a UK or EU passport photo (35×45 mm) is 413×531 pixels, and a Canadian passport photo (50×70 mm) is 591×827 pixels. This tool produces those exact pixel sizes.

What is the file size limit for a passport photo?

It depends on the portal. The US online visa photo (DS-160) must be a JPEG between 600×600 and 1200×1200 pixels and under 240 KB. Many other application portals cap uploads at 50 KB, 100 KB or 200 KB. Pick the limit your form states — this tool compresses to 50, 100 or 200 KB.

Can I use a phone photo as a passport photo?

Yes, if it meets the rules: a plain white or off-white background, your face looking straight at the camera with a neutral expression, no shadows, no glasses glare, and your head the right size in the frame (US: 1 to 1⅜ inches, 25–35 mm). Take the photo against a plain white wall in bright, even light, then use this tool to crop it to the exact passport dimensions.

What background does a passport photo need?

A plain white or off-white background with no patterns, objects or shadows. This tool resizes and compresses your photo but cannot change its background — take the picture against a plain white wall. If your photo has a busy background, retake it rather than trying to edit the background out.

Will cropping change my head size in the photo?

The tool crops from the centre of your photo to the passport aspect ratio, so frame your head in the centre when you take the picture, with some space above it. For a US photo your head (chin to top of hair) must be 1 to 1⅜ inches — 300 to 412 pixels at 300 DPI. If your head fills the whole frame, step back and retake the photo before uploading.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser using your device's processor. Your photos never leave your machine, so this is safe for passport and ID photos.
