Why a Normal Character Counter Gets X Wrong

A generic counter tells you how many characters you typed. That is the wrong number for X, because X does not count your post that way. Every link in a post is wrapped into a short t.co URL, and X charges a flat 23 characters for it — whether the link you pasted is 25 characters or 150. A draft with two long links can look 100 characters over the limit on a normal counter while fitting comfortably on X, and a draft that looks fine can hide the opposite problem if you trimmed the wrong thing.

This counter applies X's rule before it shows you the number: each URL is swapped for 23 characters, everything else counts as typed, and the result is measured against 280. The "characters typed" figure is kept alongside it, so you can see exactly how much the link rule saved you.

How X Counts a Post

  1. Start at 280. That is the limit for a standard post — the number every free account plans around.
  2. Count every link as 23 characters. X wraps links in its t.co shortener; the wrapped link always costs 23, regardless of the original length.
  3. Everything else counts as typed — letters, spaces, line breaks, hashtags, @mentions, and punctuation. A handful of special characters and some emoji can count as 2 on X, so leave a couple of characters spare if your post is full of them.
  4. Check what is left. If the X count is 280 or under, the post fits. If it is over, trim filler or split it into a thread — the tool below does the split for you.

Tips for Posts That Fit — and Get Read

  • Put the link early in your edit, not at the end of your thinking. Since a link always costs 23, write the post around it: link first, then spend the remaining ~257 characters on the hook, not the other way round.
  • Leave 5–10 characters spare. Quoting, a reply thread, or an emoji that X counts as 2 can push a post that "just fits" over the line. A small buffer saves a last-minute rewrite.
  • One or two hashtags beat a wall of them. Every hashtag counts character by character, and posts with one or two focused tags read cleaner than posts where the tags ate a third of the limit.
  • Break lines on purpose. Line breaks count, but they also make a post scannable. Spend a few characters on a break before the link or the punchline — readability is worth more than the characters it costs.
  • If it needs a thread, plan the break. End each post on a complete thought, not mid-sentence, and number the posts (1/, 2/…) so readers follow. The thread splitter below breaks at word boundaries and numbers each part for you.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trusting a generic word counter for X. It over-counts every link and can talk you out of posting a draft that fits — or, if you trimmed the link text instead of real words, into posting a weaker one.
  • Pasting a shortened link and assuming it is free. Even a bit.ly link gets re-wrapped by X and still costs 23 characters. Shortening the URL yourself does not save limit space on X.
  • Forgetting that spaces and line breaks count. A post formatted with blank lines for readability can quietly be 10–20 characters longer than the same words on one line.
  • Writing to exactly 280. A post at the exact limit leaves no room for an edit, a tag, or an emoji swap. Aim for 270 or under and keep the last few characters as insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many characters can a tweet be in 2026?

280 characters for a standard post on X (formerly Twitter). That limit has held since late 2017, when it doubled from the original 140. Longer posts exist for paying subscribers, but the free limit everyone plans around is still 280.

Do links count toward the 280-character limit?

Yes — but not by their real length. X shortens every link to a t.co URL and counts it as 23 characters, no matter how long the original link is. So a 90-character link costs you only 23 characters of your limit, which is why this counter counts each URL as 23.

Is the character counter for X different from a normal character counter?

Yes. A normal counter just counts the characters you typed, so a post with two long links looks far over the limit when it is not. An X counter applies X's own rule — links count as 23 characters each — and shows how much of the 280 limit is actually left.

Do spaces and line breaks count on X?

Yes, spaces count as characters on X, and each line break counts too. A post that looks short because it is spread over several lines can still be near the limit — the counter above counts spaces and line breaks exactly as typed.

Do hashtags and mentions count toward the limit?

Yes. Hashtags and @mentions count character by character, just like ordinary words — the # and @ symbols included. The counter above also totals your hashtags and mentions separately so you can see how many you used.

What should I do if my tweet is over 280 characters?

Cut filler words first, then shorten the link text around your URL (the URL itself still costs 23), and finally split what is left into a thread. Click "Split into a thread" above and the tool breaks your draft at word boundaries into 280-character posts, each numbered 1/, 2/, and so on.

Is my draft uploaded or stored anywhere?

No. The counting happens entirely in your browser and your draft never leaves your device. You can paste a half-finished post here, check it, and close the page — nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
