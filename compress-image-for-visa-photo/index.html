Why Visa Photos Get Rejected

Visa photos fail for a different reason than most people expect: every country wants a different size. A 35×45 mm photo that is perfect for a Schengen visa is the wrong size for a US visa (2×2 in) and for a China visa (33×48 mm — taller and narrower than 35×45). Then there is the file-size cap: the US DS-160 form rejects anything over 240 KB, China's online form wants 40–120 KB, and India's regular visa portal caps at 300 KB while its e-visa allows up to 1 MB. Add the recency rule — the US and China both require a photo taken within the last 6 months — and the glasses rule (India's e-visa refuses photos with spectacles, even prescription ones), and it is easy to see why a photo that worked for one application fails the next. This page fixes the size and the file size automatically; the tips below cover the rest.

The pixel sizes come from the print standard of 300 DPI (dots per inch). At 300 DPI, one inch is 300 pixels and one millimetre is about 11.81 pixels. So a US visa photo (2×2 inches) is 600×600 px, a Schengen visa photo (35×45 mm) is 413×531 px, a China visa photo (33×48 mm) is 390×567 px, and a Canadian visa photo (50×70 mm) is 591×827 px. Portals check these numbers, so getting them exactly right matters more than getting them roughly right.

How the Visa Photo Tool Works

  1. Pick your visa type above, then click "Choose a photo" and pick any JPEG, PNG or WebP. It starts processing the moment the photo loads.
  2. The tool crops the photo from the centre to that visa's aspect ratio, then resizes it to the exact pixel size — 600×600 for the US and India, 413×531 for Schengen and the UK, 390×567 for China, 591×827 for Canada.
  3. It then searches from high to low JPEG quality, keeping the best quality that still fits under your chosen file-size limit (50, 100, 200 or 240 KB — the 240 KB chip matches the US DS-160 maximum). The exact dimensions are never changed to hit the size — only the quality is.
  4. Check the result — size, dimensions, and a side-by-side preview — then hit "Download Visa Photo". Done in seconds.

JPEG is used for the output because PNG compression is lossless and can't hit an exact size reliably. Your photo is processed entirely in your browser: nothing is uploaded, stored, or seen by anyone.

Tips for a Visa Photo That Gets Accepted

  • Take the photo against a plain white wall. The tool resizes and compresses — it cannot change your background. A white wall in bright, even daylight is the single biggest acceptance factor, and China asks for pure white specifically.
  • Keep your head centred with space above it. The tool crops from the centre, so a head at the edge of the frame gets cut. For a US visa your head (chin to top of hair) must be 1 to 1⅜ inches — 300 to 412 pixels at 300 DPI. For Schengen it is 32–36 mm (378–425 px); for China 28–33 mm (331–390 px).
  • Use a photo taken within the last 6 months. The US and China both require it. An older photo can be rejected even if the size is perfect — take a fresh one for the visa rather than reusing an old passport photo.
  • Take your glasses off for an India e-visa. The e-visa portal refuses photos with spectacles, even clear prescription lenses. Other countries allow glasses only if there is no glare — safer to take them off for any visa photo.
  • Check your portal's limit before choosing. The US DS-160 caps at 240 KB; China's online form wants 40–120 KB; India's e-visa allows 10 KB to 1 MB but the regular visa portal stops at 300 KB. Pick the limit your form states — the 240 KB chip above matches the US maximum.
  • Start from the original photo, not a re-saved copy. Every JPEG save discards detail; compressing an already-compressed file looks worse at the same file size. Always upload the freshest copy you have.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a 35×45 mm photo for a US visa. Schengen size is not US size — the US wants a 2×2 inch square. Pick the visa type above and let the tool make the exact size for that country.
  • Mixing up China 33×48 mm with Schengen 35×45 mm. They look similar but China is taller and narrower; a 35×45 photo is already too wide for the Chinese frame. Use the China chip above for a China visa.
  • Submitting a photo older than 6 months. The US and China check recency. A photo from last year's passport application is not a visa photo, even if nothing about your face changed.
  • Wearing glasses in an India e-visa photo. The portal rejects it outright. Take the photo without spectacles.
  • Uploading your visa photo to random "free" sites. Many photo tools send your file to their server and you have no idea what happens next. This tool runs entirely on your device — check any tool for that before trusting it with an ID photo.
  • Stretching a photo to fit. Squashing a landscape photo into a square distorts the face and gets rejected. This tool crops to the right shape instead of stretching — always crop, never stretch.
  • Re-uploading the finished visa photo back through the tool. Re-encoding a compressed JPEG discards more detail each time. Compress once, from the original, and keep the master.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I compress a photo for a visa application?

Upload your photo above, pick your visa type (US, Schengen, China, India e-visa or Canada), and choose your portal's file-size limit (50, 100, 200 or 240 KB). The tool crops the photo to that visa's exact dimensions and compresses it under the limit, at the best quality that still fits.

What size should a visa photo be in pixels?

It depends on the country. At the standard print resolution of 300 DPI, a US visa photo (2×2 inches) is 600×600 pixels, a Schengen visa photo (35×45 mm) is 413×531 pixels, a China visa photo (33×48 mm) is 390×567 pixels, and a Canadian visa photo (50×70 mm) is 591×827 pixels. This tool produces those exact pixel sizes.

What is the file size limit for a US visa photo?

The US online visa photo (DS-160) must be a JPEG between 600×600 and 1200×1200 pixels and under 240 KB. Pick the 240 KB target above and the tool compresses your photo to fit, at the best quality under that limit.

What size is a China visa photo?

A China visa photo is 33×48 mm — 390×567 pixels at 300 DPI — which is taller and narrower than the 35×45 mm photo most other visas use, so a Schengen-size photo will not fit. The head must be 28–33 mm tall (331–390 px), with 3–5 mm of white space above it. The online COVA form takes a JPEG of 354–420 × 472–560 px and 40–120 KB.

What are the India e-visa photo requirements?

The India e-visa photo is a square JPEG, 2×2 inches (51×51 mm), between 10 KB and 1 MB — the regular visa portal is stricter at 10 KB to 300 KB. The digital file must be square, 350–1000 px on each side, with the head 25–35 mm tall on a plain white or light background. The e-visa photo must be taken without glasses, even prescription ones.

Can I use the same photo for my passport and my visa?

Only if the size and the rules match. A US passport photo (2×2 in) works for a US visa, but a Schengen visa photo (35×45 mm) will not work for a US visa, and a China visa needs the different 33×48 mm size. Visa photos must also be recent — taken within the last 6 months for the US and China — so an older passport photo can be rejected even if the size is right. When in doubt, make a fresh photo for the visa with this tool.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Everything happens inside your browser using your device's processor. Your photos never leave your machine, so this is safe for visa and ID photos.
