JPG to PDF Converter

Turn one photo — or a whole stack of them — into a single PDF in seconds. Pick your images, reorder them, choose A4 or original size, and download. Free, instant, 100% local in your browser · no sign-up. Perfect for scanned documents, certificates, ID photos and application attachments.

Why Convert JPG to PDF?

Photos are easy to take but awkward to submit. Application portals, employers, universities and government offices almost always ask for one PDF file, not a folder of loose photos. A PDF keeps your pages in order, opens the same on every device, prints cleanly, and can't be accidentally reordered by a gallery app. Scanned documents — ID cards, certificates, signed forms, receipts — become a single professional-looking file instead of five separate attachments.

The catch with most "free" converters: they upload your documents to somebody's server first. This page never does that. The PDF is assembled on your own device, so ID photos and certificates stay private.

How JPG to PDF Conversion Works Here

  1. Click "Choose images" and pick one or many photos — from your phone gallery or your computer.
  2. Reorder them with the ↑ and ↓ arrows so page 1 is first. Remove any photo you don't want with ✕.
  3. Pick a page size: A4 portrait is the standard for documents; Original size keeps each photo's own dimensions.
  4. Click "Download PDF" — each image becomes one page, fitted neatly inside the page with a small margin, and the file downloads immediately.

Images are re-encoded as JPEG inside the PDF at high quality (92%). PNG and WebP uploads are converted automatically, with a white background behind any transparency.

A Worked Example

You photographed both sides of your ID card and a signed form — three photos: front.jpg (2.1 MB), back.jpg (1.9 MB), form.jpg (3.4 MB) — and the application portal wants one PDF.

Upload all three, check the order (front, back, form), leave the page size on A4 portrait, and download. The result: one PDF, 3 pages, each photo fitted to an A4 page, roughly 5–6 MB total, ready to attach. Total time: under a minute, no software installed, no account created, and your ID photos never left your phone.

Tips for a Clean PDF

  • Photograph documents flat, in good light. A skewed, shadowy photo stays skewed and shadowy inside the PDF — retake beats any fix.
  • Put pages in order before downloading. Portals read page 1 first; a form before its ID card looks sloppy. Use the arrows to check the sequence.
  • Match the portal's page size. Most forms expect A4. If a portal specifies Letter (common for US applications), pick US Letter instead.
  • Compress first if the PDF is too big. If a portal caps uploads at 5 MB, shrink your photos with our image compressor before converting — the PDF will shrink with them.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uploading ID documents to random converter sites. Many converters keep a copy on their server. For personal documents, only use a tool that converts locally in your browser — like this one.
  • Screenshotting a photo to "make a PDF". Screenshots lose resolution and add screen artifacts. Convert the original photo file instead.
  • Sending one PDF per photo. Portals that ask for "one file" mean one file. Combine everything into a single PDF before uploading.
  • Forgetting page order. Galleries sort by date, not by logic. Always check the order on this page before hitting download.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a JPG to PDF?

Upload your JPG images above, arrange them in the order you want, pick a page size (A4 is the usual choice), and click Download PDF. Each image becomes one page of the PDF, and the file downloads straight to your device.

How can I convert JPG to PDF on my phone?

This page works the same on phones: open it in your mobile browser, tap Choose images, pick photos from your gallery, and download the PDF. No app install and no account needed.

Can I combine multiple JPG images into one PDF?

Yes — that is exactly what this tool is for. Add as many images as you like; they are placed in order, one per page. Use the up and down arrows on each image to reorder them before downloading.

Are my photos uploaded to a server when I convert them?

No. The PDF is built inside your browser on your own device. Your photos never leave your machine, which makes this safe for ID documents, certificates and personal photos.

What page size should I choose for a JPG to PDF conversion?

A4 portrait fits most official uses — application forms, scanned documents and certificates. Use Original size when you want each page to match the photo's own dimensions, for example for photo albums or design proofs.

Will the PDF be smaller than my photos?

Usually yes, slightly: images are re-encoded as JPEG inside the PDF at high quality, and PNG screenshots shrink a lot. If you need a much smaller file, compress your images first with our image compressor, then convert.
