How to Use the Image Compressor

  1. Click “Choose an image” and pick a photo from your device.
  2. Move the quality slider — 80% is the sweet spot for most photos.
  3. Compare the before/after sizes and previews. Lower quality means a smaller file.
  4. Click “Download Compressed Image” to save it.

When Smaller Images Help

Phone cameras now produce 3–8 MB photos, which are overkill for most uses. Marketplaces and classified sites often cap uploads at 2–5 MB, job application portals can reject anything over 1 MB, and email attachments stall above 10 MB. Website owners compress images because page speed decides search rankings — a hero photo at 200 KB instead of 3 MB can cut load time by seconds. Social apps quietly recompress your uploads anyway, so compressing first keeps you in control of quality. Because this tool works entirely in your browser, even sensitive images — IDs, documents, family photos — stay completely private.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Compression happens entirely in your browser. Your image is never uploaded, stored or shared.

Which quality setting should I use?

80% balances size and looks for photos. Use 60% for thumbnails and listings where file size matters most.

What formats are supported?

You can open JPEG, PNG and WebP files, and save as JPEG, PNG or WebP.

Why compress images at all?

Smaller images upload faster, fit form and email limits, save storage, and make web pages load much quicker.
