Why a Normal Character Counter Gets Instagram Wrong

A generic counter answers one question: how long is this text? An Instagram caption needs three answers, and the generic counter gives you none of the other two. First, is it under 2,200 characters — the hard limit Instagram enforces before it lets you post? Second, how many hashtags does it carry — because a 31st hashtag does not just get ignored, it stops the caption posting at all. Third, and most important for actually being read, what shows before the fold?

Instagram shows only about the first 125 characters of a caption in the feed, then hides the rest behind "… more". Most followers never tap it. So a caption can be under the limit and still fail: the hook, the offer, or the call to action sitting at character 400 is invisible to everyone scrolling past. This counter checks all three at once — length, hashtags, and the fold preview — so you fix the caption before you post, not after it flops.

How Instagram Counts a Caption

  1. Start at 2,200. That is the caption limit for feed posts and Reels. Your bio is a separate, much smaller limit: 150 characters.
  2. Everything counts as typed — letters, spaces, line breaks, hashtags (the # included), @mentions, punctuation and emoji.
  3. Count your hashtags separately. Instagram allows at most 30 per post. The counter warns you the moment you pass 30, before Instagram rejects the caption.
  4. Check the first ~125 characters. That is roughly what shows in the feed before "… more". Put the hook and the call to action there; hashtags and detail can live behind the fold.

A Worked Example

A bakery owner drafts a Saturday post: "Sunset sourdough, fresh out of the oven 🍞 Our Saturday bake is live — order before 6pm and skip the queue! Link in bio. #sourdough #bakery #freshbread #weekendbakes".

Paste it above and the counter shows 165 characters, 28 words, 4 hashtags, and 2,035 characters left of the 2,200 limit — comfortably inside. (The 🍞 emoji counts as 2 in the browser's count, which is why the total is 165 rather than 164.) More useful is the fold preview: the first ~125 characters carry the hook ("Sunset sourdough, fresh out of the oven…") and the call to action ("order before 6pm"), while the hashtags sit safely behind "… more" — exactly where they belong. If she adds a long story after the offer, the preview will show her whether the offer still survives the fold.

Tips for Captions That Fit — and Get Read

  • Front-load the first 125 characters. Lead with the hook or the offer, not with "Happy Saturday everyone!!" — the greeting eats the only characters guaranteed to be seen.
  • Put hashtags at the end, not in the hook. Hashtags behind the fold still work for discovery, and keeping them out of the first lines leaves room for words people actually read.
  • Stay well under 30 hashtags. Instagram allows 30, but a wall of 30 tags reads as spam. A handful of specific tags (your product, your town, your niche) beats 30 generic ones — and the counter keeps you safely under the hard limit either way.
  • Use line breaks as punctuation. Line breaks count as characters, but a break before the call to action makes it stand out in the feed. Spend a character or two on readability.
  • Check the bio separately. Bios cap at 150 characters including emoji and line breaks, so a bio that looks short can still be over. Switch the counter to Bio mode above and paste it in.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burying the offer behind "… more". If the discount, the date, or the link-in-bio prompt sits past ~125 characters, most scrollers never see it. Check the fold preview before posting.
  • Adding a 31st hashtag. One tag too many blocks the whole caption from posting — and Instagram's error does not say which tag is the problem. Count first.
  • Trusting a generic word counter. It tells you the length but not the hashtag total or the fold, so a caption that "fits" can still fail to post or fail to be read.
  • Forgetting emoji count as characters. A caption full of emoji can be dozens of characters longer than it looks. The counter above counts emoji the way the browser does.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many characters can an Instagram caption be?

2,200 characters. That is the hard limit for a caption on a feed post or Reel. If your caption is longer, Instagram will not let you post it until you shorten it — the counter above shows exactly how many characters you have left.

How many characters can an Instagram bio be?

150 characters. Your bio is the short text under your name on your profile, and it is capped at 150 characters including spaces, line breaks and emoji. Switch the counter above to Bio mode to check a bio draft against 150.

How many hashtags can you use on Instagram?

30 hashtags per post. Instagram allows up to 30 hashtags in a single caption or comment; a 31st hashtag makes the whole caption fail to post. The counter above totals your hashtags and warns you the moment you pass 30.

Why does my Instagram caption get cut off with '… more'?

Instagram shows only about the first 125 characters of a caption in the feed — roughly the first three lines — and hides the rest behind a '… more' tap. That is why the hook and the call to action belong in the first 125 characters, and the hashtags can safely sit at the end. The counter above previews exactly what shows before the fold.

Do hashtags, mentions and emoji count toward the Instagram character limit?

Yes. Everything in the caption counts character by character: letters, spaces, line breaks, hashtags (including the #), @mentions and emoji. Emoji count as characters too, so a caption full of emoji can be much closer to the limit than it looks.

What happens if my caption is over 2,200 characters?

Instagram refuses to post it and asks you to shorten the caption. The usual fix is to cut filler words, move extra detail into the first comment, or split a long story across a carousel's slides instead of one giant caption.

Is my caption uploaded or stored anywhere?

No. The counting happens entirely in your browser and your draft never leaves your device. You can paste an unpublished caption here, check it, and close the page — nothing is saved or sent anywhere.
